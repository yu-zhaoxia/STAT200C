Statistical Inference in Linear Models

Throughout this section we assume \[ Y=X\beta+\epsilon, \qquad \epsilon\sim N(0,\sigma^2I_n), \] where (X) is an (np) design matrix with full column rank.

Statistical inference in linear models includes estimation, confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, and model comparison. In this chapter we focus primarily on hypothesis testing for linear constraints of the form \[ A\beta=c. \]

Hypothesis Testing

Theorem 1 (Extra Sum of Squares for a General Linear Hypothesis) Suppose we test \[ H_0:A\beta=c, \] where (A) is a (qp) matrix with ((A)=q).

(a) Restricted and Full Model RSS Decomposition

\[\begin{eqnarray*} RSS_H-RSS &=& RSS_{restricted}-RSS_{full}\\ &=& \|\hat{Y}-\hat{Y}_H\|^2 \\ &=& (A\hat{\beta}-c)^T[A(X^TX)^{-1}A^T]^{-1}(A\hat{\beta}-c)\\ &=& Z^TB^{-1}Z, \end{eqnarray*}\]

where \[ Z\sim N_q(A\beta-c,\sigma^2B), \]

and \[ B=A(X^TX)^{-1}A^T. \]

Furthermore, (RSS_H-RSS) measures the amount of variation explained by relaxing the constraints (A=c).

(b) Expected Value of (RSS_H-RSS)

\[ E[RSS_H-RSS] = \sigma^2q + (A\beta-c)^T[A(X^TX)^{-1}A^T]^{-1}(A\beta-c). \]

(c) F-statistic for Testing (H_0:A=c)

Under (H_0),

\[\begin{eqnarray*} F &=& \frac{(RSS_H-RSS)/q}{RSS/(n-p)} \\ &=& \frac{ (A\hat{\beta}-c)^T[A(X^TX)^{-1}A^T]^{-1}(A\hat{\beta}-c) }{ qS^2 } \sim F_{q,n-p}. \end{eqnarray*}\]

The F-statistic compares the reduction in residual sum of squares achieved by moving from the restricted model to the full model, relative to the residual variation left unexplained by the full model.

(d) Projection Matrix Representation

When (c=0),

\[ F= \frac{n-p}{q} \frac{ Y^T(P_X-P_H)Y }{ Y^T(I-P_X)Y }, \]

where (P_H) is symmetric and idempotent, and \[ P_HP_X=P_XP_H=P_H. \]

The matrix (P_H) is the projection matrix onto the restricted model space \[ \{X\beta:A\beta=0\}. \]

Proof (Proof for (a)). Notice that

\[ Y-\hat Y \perp \mathcal C(X), \]

and

\[ \hat Y-\hat Y_H\in\mathcal C(X). \]

Therefore,

\[ (Y-\hat Y)^T(\hat Y-\hat Y_H)=0. \]

Thus,

\[\begin{eqnarray*} RSS_H &=& \|Y-\hat Y_H\|^2 \\ &=& \|Y-\hat Y+\hat Y-\hat Y_H\|^2 \\ &=& \|Y-\hat Y\|^2 + \|\hat Y-\hat Y_H\|^2 \\ &=& RSS+\|X(\hat\beta-\hat\beta_H)\|^2. \end{eqnarray*}\]

Hence,

\[\begin{eqnarray*} RSS_H-RSS &=& \|X(\hat\beta-\hat\beta_H)\|^2 \\ &=& (A\hat{\beta}-c)^T [A(X^TX)^{-1}A^T]^{-1} (A\hat{\beta}-c). \end{eqnarray*}\]

Proof for (b)

Because

\[ \hat\beta \sim N_p(\beta,\sigma^2(X^TX)^{-1}), \]

we have

\[ A\hat\beta \sim N_q \left( A\beta, \sigma^2A(X^TX)^{-1}A^T \right). \]

Let

\[ Z=A\hat\beta-c. \]

Then

\[ Z\sim N_q(A\beta-c,\sigma^2B), \]

where

\[ B=A(X^TX)^{-1}A^T. \]

Therefore,

\[\begin{eqnarray*} E[RSS_H-RSS] &=& E[Z^TB^{-1}Z] \\ &=& tr(\sigma^2B^{-1}B) + (A\beta-c)^TB^{-1}(A\beta-c) \\ &=& \sigma^2q + (A\beta-c)^TB^{-1}(A\beta-c). \end{eqnarray*}\]

Recall that if \[ Y\sim N(\mu,\Sigma), \]

then

\[ E[Y^TAY] = tr(A\Sigma)+\mu^TA\mu. \]

Proof for (c)

First,

\[\begin{eqnarray*} \frac{RSS}{\sigma^2} &=& \frac{(Y-X\hat\beta)^T(Y-X\hat\beta)}{\sigma^2} \\ &=& \frac{Y^T(I-P_X)Y}{\sigma^2} \\ &\sim& \chi^2_{n-p}. \end{eqnarray*}\]

This follows because (I-P_X) is symmetric and idempotent with rank (n-p).

Under the null hypothesis,

\[ Z\sim N_q(0,\sigma^2B), \]

and therefore

\[ \frac{RSS_H-RSS}{\sigma^2} = \frac{Z^TB^{-1}Z}{\sigma^2} \sim \chi_q^2. \]

It remains to show that (RSS_H-RSS) and (RSS) are independent.

Notice that

\[ RSS=Y^T(I-P_X)Y, \]

and

\[ RSS_H-RSS=Y^TP_1Y, \]

where

\[ P_1= X(X^TX)^{-1}A^T [A(X^TX)^{-1}A^T]^{-1} A(X^TX)^{-1}X^T. \]

Since

\[ (I-P_X)P_1=0, \]

the two quadratic forms are independent.

Therefore,

\[ F= \frac{(RSS_H-RSS)/q}{RSS/(n-p)} \sim F_{q,n-p}. \]

Proof for (d)

When (c=0),

\[\begin{eqnarray*} \hat Y_H &=& X\hat\beta_H \\ &=& \left( P_X - X(X^TX)^{-1}A^T [A(X^TX)^{-1}A^T]^{-1} A(X^TX)^{-1}X^T \right)Y \\ &=& (P_X-P_1)Y \\ &=& P_HY. \end{eqnarray*}\]

Since

\[ P_1P_X=P_XP_1=P_1, \]

we have

\[\begin{eqnarray*} (P_X-P_1)^2 &=& P_X^2-P_XP_1-P_1P_X+P_1^2 \\ &=& P_X-P_1. \end{eqnarray*}\]

Therefore (P_H=P_X-P_1) is idempotent.

Also,

\[\begin{eqnarray*} RSS_H &=& \|Y-\hat Y_H\|^2 \\ &=& Y^T(I-P_H)^2Y \\ &=& Y^T(I-P_H)Y. \end{eqnarray*}\]

Hence,

\[ RSS_H-RSS = Y^T(P_X-P_H)Y. \]

Noncentral F Distribution

Under the alternative hypothesis (Ac),

\[ \frac{RSS_H-RSS}{\sigma^2} \sim \chi_q^2(\lambda), \]

where the noncentrality parameter is

\[ \lambda= \frac{ (A\beta-c)^T[A(X^TX)^{-1}A^T]^{-1}(A\beta-c) }{ \sigma^2 }. \]

Therefore,

\[ F\sim F_{q,n-p}(\lambda). \]

Relationship Between t-tests and F-tests

When (q=1), the F-test reduces to the square of a t-test:

\[ F_{1,n-p}=t_{n-p}^2. \]

Thus the t-test is a special case of the general linear hypothesis test.
